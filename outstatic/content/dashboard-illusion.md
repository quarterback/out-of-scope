The dashboard is not a window into governance. It’s a mirror of simulation.

We live in a time where performance replaces presence—where legitimacy is conveyed not through accountability or responsiveness, but through the smooth choreography of interface panels, OKRs, and update logs.

Dashboards offer the illusion of control while obscuring the structural degradation beneath. The more detailed the display, the more convincing the narrative.

But metrics are not memory. And systems that rely on aesthetic legibility can’t handle real-world friction.

We design dashboards as if they’re neutral, but they are never neutral. They are performances of authority designed to suppress questions, to compress complexity, and to offload consequence onto whatever breaks next.
